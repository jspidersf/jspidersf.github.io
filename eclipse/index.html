The trip

On Monday 2 August 2027, the moon's shadow crosses southern Spain in the late morning. The path of totality runs from the Atlantic coast near Cádiz, across the Costa del Sol, and east past Málaga to the Costa Tropical: long beaches, a warm sea, and August mornings that are usually clear and dry. The sun will be 38–41° up, just south of east, and totality lasts from about 1½ minutes in the east to over 4 minutes in the west, around 10:45–10:52am local time.

Six of us go: two parents, children 6½ and 8½, and two grandmothers. The plan is to fly into Málaga and settle into one town for 5–8 nights. Eclipse morning needs no expedition — a beach, promenade, or hotel terrace facing the sea is enough. The rest of the week is a normal beach holiday: pools, old towns, day trips.

There are three ways to stay. A family resort hotel puts everything on site — kids' club, pools, meals. The strongest are the Don Carlos in Marbella, the Iberostar Waves Málaga Playa in Torrox Costa, and the Protur Roquetas in Roquetas de Mar. A private villa gives us our own pool and more space in a quieter setting; the strongest are a four-bedroom villa in Calahonda/Mijas and Villa Royal in Salobreña. A town-center apartment puts us in a historic center — Málaga, Cádiz, or Marbella — within walking distance of everything; the strongest are the Aduana Presidential in Málaga, a three-bedroom in Cádiz old town, and a three-bedroom in Marbella center.

Where the sun will be

At totality, the sun sits at about 38–41° altitude and 96–98° azimuth everywhere on this coast — just south of due east, out over the sea.

The obstruction rule, in plain words. At that height, a building or headland can only block the view if it is both tall and very close: something of height h metres blocks the sun only if it stands within about h ÷ 0.78 of the viewer. A 20 m building would have to be within about 26 m; a 45 m tower within about 58 m. Anything farther away physically cannot reach high enough to get in the way — the risk is a wall or building right next to where you stand, not a hill or a row of hotels down the beach.

Regional terrain, including Gibraltar, blocks no candidate beach. The Rock of Gibraltar (about 426 m) sits south-southwest of the westernmost beaches (Torreguadiaro, Sotogrande, Alcaidesa), while the sun at totality is east-southeast — opposite sides of the sky. Even setting geometry aside, the Rock's angular height from those distances is only about 1.2–1.9°, far below the sun's ~38°. The same logic clears every other headland checked (Rincón de la Victoria's Cantal Alto, La Herradura's bay). What remains unchecked everywhere is a building or wall right at a specific standing spot — a local, not a regional, question.

Viewing spots checked

Module F checked three actual viewing points on the ground, for the four Fuengirola/Benalmádena finalist properties, using OpenStreetMap building data out to 500 m toward the sun:

  • Playa de San Francisco (36.5447, -4.6172), Leonardo Hotel's beach in Los Boliches — 243 m walk, no buildings at all found within 500 m toward the sun.
  • Playa de Santa Amalia (36.5364, -4.6215), for MalagaSuite in Fuengirola — no buildings found within 500 m toward the sun. The measured 710 m walk is unreliable: it used an approximate location, and the apartment is on the seafront promenade.
  • Playa Malapesquera (36.5949, -4.5181), shared by Best Triton and the Diana 2 apartment in Benalmádena — 18–100 m walk; eight buildings sit in that direction within 500 m, but the nearest is 297 m away and even the tallest tagged one (30 m) would need to be within about 38 m to block anything.

Not yet done for La Cala, Estepona, Almuñécar, Sabinillas, or Torrox. No equivalent walkable-viewpoint-plus-building check has been run for these five places. The coast-wide geometry above still applies, but no specific spot in any of them has been checked against mapped buildings, and OSM's height tagging is incomplete everywhere, so an untagged tall structure can't be fully ruled out at any point, checked or not.

Traffic and crowds

Plan no driving on 2 August, especially in the hour or two after totality ends (around 10:50–10:51 local). During Spain's 12 August 2026 eclipse, traffic on Catalonia's AP-7 motorway backed up about 10 km despite access controls. The DGT (Spain's traffic authority) named the post-totality return trip the highest-risk period and told drivers not to stop on the shoulder. None of this is a 2027-specific plan; the DGT is expected to publish one only close to the event.

Beaches are public and need no reservation. Under Spain's Ley de Costas, beaches are public and free to use for walking, bathing and standing. Expect heavy ordinary August crowding on top of eclipse demand — none of the checked viewing points is a designated eclipse venue, so arriving well before first contact to claim space is sensible.

Weather

Eclipse-day cloud cover cannot be forecast eleven months out. What exists is long-run climatology (2000–2024 satellite and ground data), which favours the Mediterranean (Alborán) coast over Spain's Atlantic side: one source gives Cádiz about 88% sun-visible days against 74% for the more Atlantic-exposed Barbate/Tarifa/Zahara group, and states that Atlantic cloudiness usually doesn't push past Tarifa into the Strait. Gibraltar is a partial exception, with morning marine stratus that usually burns off within an hour or two. No town-by-town comparison exists east of Marbella, so nothing here separates Fuengirola from Almuñécar on weather grounds. Climatology is not a forecast for 2027-08-02 specifically.

Eye safety

Only glasses meeting the ISO 12312-2:2015 standard are safe for direct viewing. Before the 12 August 2026 eclipse, Spain's consumer-protection ministry ordered several models of eclipse glasses withdrawn from sale over injury risk (the research notes record seven; an early press report said six). Some were on ordinary retail sale, not only from obscure sellers. Buy from a reputable supplier well ahead of time and check that the CE mark, the ISO 12312-2:2015 marking, the manufacturer's name, and instructions are all present on the glasses themselves, not just the packaging.

Costs the room price leaves out

None of the accommodation figures above include these (dated benchmarks, not quotes, for the party of six):

  • Airport transfers. A pre-booked minivan is about €62–79 each way to Fuengirola and about €68–70 each way to Benalmádena (2026 prices) — roughly €125–160 round trip. A standard Málaga taxi is licensed for four, so six people need two taxis or one larger pre-booked vehicle, costing about the same as the minivan.
  • No tourist tax. Andalusia levies no regional tourist tax, and the current PP–Vox regional government has explicitly ruled one out. A change before 2027 is possible but not indicated so far.
  • Eclipse glasses. ISO 12312-2 glasses for six run roughly €18–30, depending on supplier and pack size (2026 retail prices).
  • Groceries, self-catering options. Module F priced groceries for this party at roughly €250–400 for a 9-night stay; scaled to 5–8 nights, roughly €140–355 — this guide's own scaling, not a separately sourced figure. No comparable eating-out figure exists for the hotel options, beyond the general benchmark that a Spanish sit-down lunch (menú del día) commonly runs €12–20 per person.

How places were chosen

Areas are judged in this order: kid compatibility and parental ease first, then grandma access, then price, then Mediterranean setting, then weather, with totality duration last. (Jeremy, 2026-09-21.) This replaced the earlier order that ranked grandma access first.

The family bases on the Mediterranean side — the Costa del Sol and, as a second tier, the Costa Tropical near Almuñécar — plus the Cádiz/Strait coast. The Atlantic side (Vejer and the towns near it) was set aside when the family chose the Mediterranean.

Fifteen towns have active pages in this guide. The places below were considered and not carried forward. Reserve places are kept as substitutes if a leading area falls through; dropped places were ruled out on their own merits.

Reserve

  • La Carihuela (Torremolinos) — 2m48s. A substitute for Fuengirola: flat promenade and fish restaurants near a C1 train stop, but a hill or stairs stand between the promenade and the town centre and some stations.
  • Rincón de la Victoria / Torre del Mar — 1m57s / 1m42s. Close to Málaga on logistics alone, but neither offers a different kind of holiday from the central options already ahead of them.
  • Torreguadiaro — 4m02s. Quiet and long totality, but everyday services are thin. (Sotogrande itself now has an active page.)

Dropped

  • La Herradura — 1m48s. A sheltered bay, but visitor reports consistently describe a pebble beach that deepens within a few metres of the shore, which is poor for young children and grandmothers.
  • Alcaidesa — 4m10s. A well-equipped beach, but a real-estate source calls a car "practically essential" there, and the measured route from the plaza to the beach is 1.45 km with an 82 m climb back.
  • La Línea — totality not recorded for the beach in this research. Long totality was its one point in favour, but no family advantage was found.
  • Cabo de Gata — San José is total, at about 1m30s, but services are sparse and access is remote, which suits neither children nor grandmothers; La Isleta del Moro sits at the edge of the path of totality and its status there is undetermined.
  • Vejer de la Frontera (with Zahara de los Atunes) — the original inland-plus-beach pick. Set aside when the family chose the Mediterranean side and revised the priorities (Jeremy, 2026-09-19): a walled hill town with no sea view loses on kid compatibility and parental ease first.
