The trip

About two weeks in summer 2027: a week near Nazaré in Portugal and a week on the south coast of Spain. Six of us: four adults and two children, who will be 6½ and 8½. We'd fly from San Francisco together. Nothing is booked yet.

The Spain week is built around the total solar eclipse on the morning of Monday 2 August 2027. At roughly 10:47–10:49 a.m. local time, depending on the town, the moon will completely cover the sun. Totality lasts about 1½–3½ minutes across the main places compared here. The sun will be about 40 degrees up in the eastern sky, over the sea, so a beach near where we stay works as a viewing spot. We plan to walk there that morning rather than drive. After Spain's August 2026 eclipse, traffic on one motorway backed up about 10 km.

The leading flight plan is Portugal first, from 21 July, then Spain from 29 July to 5 August. See Flights below.

Where the sun will be

Research updated 2026-10-22. Prices retain their original check dates.

Regional calculations place the sun roughly 38–41° above the eastern horizon at totality. Exact direction and altitude depend on the standing point.

A simple obstruction estimate uses obstacle height above the observer, horizontal distance, sun direction and terrain elevation. On level ground, angular height is arctan(height difference / distance); that approximation cannot clear every hill or coast-wide viewpoint. Incomplete mapped building heights and an unmeasured local approach remain material gaps. Verify the actual standing point before treating it as unobstructed.

Traffic and crowds

Research updated 2026-10-22. Prices retain their original check dates.

Plan no driving on 2 August, especially in the hour or two after totality ends (around 10:50–10:51 local). During Spain's 12 August 2026 eclipse, traffic on Catalonia's AP-7 motorway backed up about 10 km despite access controls. The DGT (Spain's traffic authority) named the post-totality return trip the highest-risk period and told drivers not to stop on the shoulder. None of this is a 2027-specific plan; the DGT is expected to publish one only close to the event.

Beaches are public and need no reservation. Under Spain's Ley de Costas, beaches are public and free to use for walking, bathing and standing. Expect heavy ordinary August crowding on top of eclipse demand — none of the checked viewing points is a designated eclipse venue, so arriving well before first contact to claim space is sensible.

Eye safety

Research updated 2026-10-22. Prices retain their original check dates.

Use undamaged solar viewers from an AAS-listed supplier or its identified authorized reseller. ISO markings and brand names can be counterfeited; labels alone do not establish safety. Follow AAS supplier and viewer guidance.

Use safe viewers during every partial phase. Only within the path, while the Sun is completely covered in true totality, may you remove them; replace them before any bright solar surface reappears. Supervise children. Never look through binoculars, a telescope or a camera optical finder while wearing eclipse glasses. Optical equipment needs appropriate solar filters secured over the front; see AAS viewing guidance.

For the central Costa del Sol towns under consideration (Fuengirola, Benalmádena, Torremolinos), the choice is a pre-booked minivan, two standard taxis, or the Renfe C1 Cercanías train.
